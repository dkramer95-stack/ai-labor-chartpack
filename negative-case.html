The negative case — where AI exposure is actually costing jobs

BLS data through Sep 2026 · evidence DOIs resolved via OpenAlex · built 1 Nov 2026
Read this first: total payrolls are still above their 2019 level and grew 0.3% over the past year. Nothing here is an economy-wide collapse. The negative case is real but sectoral and market-specific — information, entry-level hiring, and freelance task markets. Claiming more than that is over-reading the data.

The evidence, ranked — counter-evidence kept in the table

#PaperDirectionWhat it shows
1The Short-Term Effects of Generative AI on Employment: Evidence from an Online Labor Market
Hui, Reshef, Zhou · 2023 · SSRN
negative evidenceAfter ChatGPT's release, freelancers in more-exposed Upwork occupations had fewer monthly jobs and lower earnings than less-exposed occupations. The cleanest displacement result in the stack, in the narrowest market.
2Who is AI Replacing? The Impact of Generative AI on Online Freelancing Platforms
Demirci, Hannane · 2024 · SSRN
negative evidenceFreelance demand fell sharply in writing and graphic-design categories after image and text models launched; the decline is concentrated in exactly the tasks models do well.
3Artificial Intelligence and the Labor Market
Hampole, Papanikolaou et al. · 2025 · NBER Working Paper 33509
negative evidenceFirm-level AI investment is associated with weaker hiring, with the burden falling on entry-level and lower-skill roles rather than on total headcount.
4AI exposure predicts unemployment risk: A new approach to technology-driven job loss
Frank, Ahn · 2025 · PNAS Nexus
negative evidenceExposure scores carry information about unemployment risk, though individual scores alone are weak predictors. Best read as evidence that exposure is not noise.
5Automation and New Tasks: How Technology Displaces and Reinstates Labor
Acemoglu, Restrepo · 2019 · Journal of Economic Perspectives
frameworkWhy displacement claims must be paired with reinstatement. The negative case has to survive this frame, not ignore it.
6Artificial Intelligence and Jobs: Evidence from Online Vacancies
Acemoglu, Autor, Hazell, Restrepo · 2022 · Journal of Labor Economics
mixedAI-exposed establishments post more AI vacancies without clear total employment declines — the strongest counterweight to the freelancer results.
7Rethinking Automation Risk: AI Applicability and Occupational Outcomes, 2019-24
Broady, Dunson · 2026 · Working paper (doi 10.21033)
counter-evidenceOccupations with higher AI applicability saw employment and wages grow over 2019-24. Directly contradicts the displacement thesis at the occupation level.
8GPTs are GPTs: An Early Look at the Labor Market Impact Potential of Large Language Models
Eloundou, Manning, Mishkin, Rock · 2023 · arXiv
measurementThe exposure index everything else is built on. Capability overlap, not adoption — treat it as a hypothesis generator.
